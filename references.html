Reading list

Every source the series relies on, grouped by topic: everything cited on the pages so far, plus the papers later pages will build on. Each entry was checked against the paper itself or its publisher. Entries cited on a page link back to where they're used.

The list grows as the series does, and the goal is for it to cover the literature on its own. If you know of work that belongs here, please open an issue on GitHub.

Software independence and voting machine security

Why audits rely on paper: studies of what voting software can be made to do.

  • Ronald L. Rivest, "On the notion of 'software independence' in voting systems," Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A 366(1881):3759–3767, 2008. doi:10.1098/rsta.2008.0149. An earlier version, written with John P. Wack for NIST, dates from 2006: NIST copy. Cited in Part 0
  • Tadayoshi Kohno, Adam Stubblefield, Aviel D. Rubin and Dan S. Wallach, "Analysis of an Electronic Voting System," IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy, 2004. Cited in Part 0
  • David Wagner, David Jefferson and Matt Bishop, "Security Analysis of the Diebold AccuBasic Interpreter," Voting Systems Technology Assessment Advisory Board, report for the California Secretary of State, February 14, 2006. It confirms the memory-card attack demonstrated by Harri Hursti in 2005. Cited in Part 0
  • Matt Bishop, "Overview of Red Team Reports," California Secretary of State Top-to-Bottom Review of voting systems, July 2007. Systems examined: Diebold (GEMS/AccuVote), Hart InterCivic (System 6.2.1) and Sequoia (WinEDS/Edge/Insight/400-C). Cited in Part 0
  • Patrick McDaniel, Matt Blaze, Giovanni Vigna et al., EVEREST: Evaluation and Validation of Election-Related Equipment, Standards and Testing, Final Report, for the Ohio Secretary of State, December 7, 2007. Systems examined: ES&S, Hart InterCivic and Premier (formerly Diebold). Cited in Part 0
  • Ariel J. Feldman, J. Alex Halderman and Edward W. Felten, "Security Analysis of the Diebold AccuVote-TS Voting Machine," USENIX/ACCURATE Electronic Voting Technology Workshop (EVT '07), 2007. Cited in Part 0
  • Andrew W. Appel, Maia Ginsburg, Harri Hursti, Brian W. Kernighan, Christopher D. Richards and Gang Tan, Insecurities and Inaccuracies of the Sequoia AVC Advantage 9.00H DRE Voting Machine, expert report in Gusciora v. Corzine (N.J. Super. Ct.), October 2008. Cited in Part 0
  • Stephen Checkoway, Ariel J. Feldman, Brian Kantor, J. Alex Halderman, Edward W. Felten and Hovav Shacham, "Can DREs Provide Long-Lasting Security? The Case of Return-Oriented Programming and the AVC Advantage," EVT/WOTE '09, 2009. Cited in Part 0
  • Scott Wolchok, Eric Wustrow, J. Alex Halderman, Hari K. Prasad, Arun Kankipati, Sai Krishna Sakhamuri, Vasavya Yagati and Rop Gonggrijp, "Security Analysis of India's Electronic Voting Machines," ACM Conference on Computer and Communications Security (CCS '10), 2010. Cited in Part 0
  • Matt Blaze, Harri Hursti, Margaret MacAlpine, Mary Hanley, Jeff Moss, Rachel Wehr, Kendall Spencer and Christopher Ferris, DEF CON 27 Voting Machine Hacking Village Report, September 2019. Cited in Part 0
  • Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, "Vulnerabilities Affecting Dominion Voting Systems ImageCast X," advisory ICSA-22-154-01, June 2022. Vulnerabilities reported by J. Alex Halderman and Drew Springall. Cited in Part 0
  • Matthew Bernhard, Allison McDonald, Henry Meng, Jensen Hwa, Nakul Bajaj, Kevin Chang and J. Alex Halderman, "Can Voters Detect Malicious Manipulation of Ballot Marking Devices?" IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy, 2020. Cited in Part 0
  • Andrew W. Appel, Richard A. DeMillo and Philip B. Stark, "Ballot-Marking Devices Cannot Ensure the Will of the Voters," Election Law Journal 19(3):432–450, 2020. doi:10.1089/elj.2019.0619. Cited in Part 0
  • Matthew Bernhard, Kartikeya Kandula, Jeremy Wink and J. Alex Halderman, "UnclearBallot: Automated Ballot Image Manipulation," E-Vote-ID 2019, pp. 14–31. Cited in Part 0

Hand counts and recounts

How accurate human counting is, in the lab and in real recounts.

  • Stephen N. Goggin, Michael D. Byrne and Juan E. Gilbert, "Post-Election Auditing: Effects of Procedure and Ballot Type on Manual Counting Accuracy, Efficiency, and Auditor Satisfaction and Confidence," Election Law Journal 11(1):36–51, 2012. doi:10.1089/elj.2010.0098. The figures above are from its Table 1 (candidate count errors: 1.4% overall, 0.48% read-and-mark, 2.13% sort-and-stack) and results section (46.7% of groups miscounted at least one candidate). Cited in Part 0
  • Lonna Rae Atkeson, R. Michael Alvarez and Thad E. Hall, The New Mexico 2006 Post Election Audit Report, revised October 2009. Part III, pp. 42–43 and 49–64: average differences between the first machine count and the two- and three-person hand counts of 0.19% and 0.13%; 76% of Election Day batches identical between machine and hand count (52% absentee, 61% early); the two hand counts identical in over 70% of Election Day and early batches and 45% of absentee batches, with no batch off by more than 6 ballots (pp. 62–63, Tables 3.7–3.8); the authors write that over- and under-counting "does not favor one candidate or party over another." Cited in Part 0
  • Stephen Ansolabehere and Andrew Reeves, "Using Recounts to Measure the Accuracy of Vote Tabulations: Evidence from New Hampshire Elections, 1946–2002," Caltech/MIT Voting Technology Project Working Paper 11, 2004, p. 8. Cited in Part 0
  • Stephen Ansolabehere, Barry C. Burden, Kenneth R. Mayer and Charles Stewart III, "Learning from Recounts," Election Law Journal 17(2):100–116, 2018. doi:10.1089/elj.2017.0440. Cited in Part 0
  • Walter R. Mebane, Jr. and Matthew Bernhard, "Voting Technologies, Recount Methods and Votes in Wisconsin and Michigan in 2016," 3rd Workshop on Advances in Secure Electronic Voting (Voting '18), Financial Cryptography and Data Security, 2018. Table 5. Cited in Part 0
