A series of interactive explainers

Risk-limiting audits from the ground up

After an election, how do you check that the reported winner really won, without counting every ballot by hand? A risk-limiting audit looks at a random sample of paper ballots, and keeps looking until the sample makes a strong enough case, or until every ballot has been counted.

These pages build up the reasoning behind risk-limiting audits one step at a time, for curious people who don't have a background in statistics. Each one uses the same small election: Alice against Bob.
